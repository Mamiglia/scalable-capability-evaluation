The saturated-item baseline is the reference protocol (its protocol effect is fixed at zero), and the domain effects sum to zero. The protocol-by-domain deviations are partially pooled separately for each protocol, drawn from a normal distribution with a protocol-specific standard deviation $\sigma_p$, which measures how much that protocol's effect varies across domains. The target-by-domain term is a random intercept that accounts for every protocol predicting the same target in the same domain, and each protocol has its own Gamma shape. Priors were $\mathcal{N}(0,2)$ on the intercept, $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ on the protocol and domain effects, $\operatorname{HalfNormal}(0.5)$ on $\sigma_p$, $\operatorname{HalfNormal}(1)$ on the standard deviation of the target-by-domain intercepts, and $\operatorname{Exponential}(1)$ on the shapes (four chains, 1,000 warm-up and 2,000 retained draws each; no divergences, $\hat{R} \leq 1.004$, bulk effective sample size $\geq 905$). The exponentiated protocol effect gives a protocol's pooled MSE ratio relative to the baseline, and the exponentiated sum of the protocol effect and its domain deviation gives its ratio in that domain; we report posterior medians and 95\% credible intervals.
